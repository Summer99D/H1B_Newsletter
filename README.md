#### My thought process

I started the assessment with the reasers of the newsletter in mind. I don't know what portion of it currently is HR specialists or job-seekers but my goal was to speak about a topic human enough those groups would understand, without having an economics degree. I wanted the newsletter to give them insight and make them think: "Ah this is so cool I want Revelio lab to help me derive similar insights".

I wanted to do an analysis not purely for a newsletter that economists would read but one that does two things:

1. Gives us (as revelio labs) an insight into where we can find potential clients (those that still struggling with high ATF for roles can definitely benefit from our psuite of products)

2. Those who read our newsletter and are actually in the HR industry. 

You see, these newsletters for people like us data scientists and economists is like reading AD is for an architect: it definitely gives insight, but it is something we already know how to find. 

But our clients don't know what they can do with data, what they can do with our products and what we can help them see in the "Blind spot" 
And so, although I am not an economist and despite knwoing that this model needs a lot of improvement, I wanted to submit this!

Originally the way I was hoping to approach this assessment, was to find key points in time so that I could do and RDD to begin things with. Important dates that I had in mind would have been 

- Russia-Ukraine war
- AI boom
- USA elections (and results 2024)
- EO on H1B
- Iran-Israel-US war

Sadly, the data did not have most of these so I ended up settling for those present. I did not get into the elections and war as they were macro phenomenans that people do not have a control on. for that purpose I decided to go with AI boom. 

If I want this newsletter to actually have data backing, I was going to to time series from before AI boom (november 2022) so we can see how it has changed over time. I was trying to see whether AI has actually made job-markets and job-turnover faster (by decreaging posting, applying and hiring processtime) or has it just made everything more complicated? 

By complicated I mean increasing speed of applying so the recruiters have become overwhelmed. I don't know if this is acheivable or not but it would have been interesting if we could measure how the speed of rejection has changed with AI (ATS tracking systems)

But again, the data was very limited (2022-2025) so despite knowing that I cannot draw any concrete conclusions, I wrote about the things I found.
